A different kind of practice

Small by design, so care can be timely and personal

Treatment starts with a thorough evaluation to identify the correct psychiatric diagnosis, as well as the biological, environmental, social, and other factors that may contribute to it. Lab tests are often used to assist in diagnosis and treatment monitoring. The results become a personalized treatment plan built around each patient’s values and preferences.

Our practice is intentionally small: clients are seen as frequently as needed to treat their conditions, allowing timely treatment adjustments and a faster return to health. Care is coordinated with primary care physicians, pediatricians, and therapists to ensure the best outcome.

Thorough Evaluation

Initial evaluations are a full 120 minutes — enough time to truly understand the problem, review history in depth, and discuss the initial treatment approach together.

Integrated Treatment

Therapy, nutrition, medications, or a combination — an integrated approach that recognizes the real connections between body, mind, family, and community.

Coordinated Care

Care is coordinated with your other providers — PCPs, pediatricians, and therapists — so treatment works as one plan, not several competing ones.

Metabolic & nutritional psychiatry

Nutrition is part of mental health

Dr. Deister has advanced training from the Walsh Research Institute in using nutrition — vitamins, minerals, and other supplements — to treat psychiatric and developmental disorders, alongside or instead of medications when appropriate.

For clients who wish to use nutritional approaches, lab testing helps target treatment precisely; these tests are often covered by insurance. She also has experience with dietary approaches for mental health, including elimination, gluten-free, dairy-free, low-carb, and ketogenic diets.

Meet the doctor

Diana Deister, MD, MS

A child, adolescent, adult, and family psychiatrist, Dr. Deister trained at UT Health San Antonio and Brown University, then taught and practiced for a decade at Boston Children’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School — twice earning its Goldman Teaching Award in Psychiatry — before founding Bountiful Mental Health in 2023.

She sees patients of all ages by telehealth across Massachusetts, with limited in-person availability in Brookline.
